How to Find Local American Mahjong Groups, Teachers and Tournaments

Start with libraries, community and recreation centers, and Jewish Community Centers, then check Meetup, Facebook groups and neighborhood apps. For lessons, look at adult education programs and teacher directories.

Updated September 2026 / 3 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

On this page

  1. Places that often host open play
  2. Finding groups online
  3. Finding a teacher or class
  4. Tournaments
  5. Before your first local game

Online play is great for practice, but American Mahjong is at its best in person. The community is generally welcoming to newcomers. You just need to know where to look. (More social guides: Around the table.)

Places that often host open play

  • Public libraries. Many run game days or regular Mahjong meetups, and they tend to be relaxed and beginner-friendly. Check the events calendar.
  • Jewish Community Centers and synagogues. American Mahjong has deep roots in Jewish-American communities, and many JCCs and synagogues host regular games or lessons. Some welcome non-members, sometimes for a small fee.
  • Senior centers and recreation centers. Mahjong often appears on adult program schedules alongside bridge and canasta.
  • YMCAs and parks departments. Look through adult program listings.
  • Clubs. Country clubs and social clubs often have regular card-room games. If you know a member, ask whether guests are welcome.
  • Game stores and cafes. Some board game stores host Mahjong nights or know who does.
?

Beginner tip: When you call or email, ask directly: "Do you have a beginner-friendly game?" Many groups have a separate table or time for new players.

Finding groups online

  • Facebook groups. Search for "Mahjong" or "Mah Jongg" with your city or state. Large national American Mahjong groups can also be a good place to ask about games in your area.
  • Meetup. Search for American Mahjong or Mah Jongg. Groups often meet at cafes, libraries or food halls.
  • Neighborhood apps. A short post on an app like Nextdoor asking whether anyone hosts a beginner-friendly game can lead to invitations to private home games.
  • Group-finder tools. Some American Mahjong websites let players find or post local groups. I Love Mahj, for example, has a group-finding feature.

Finding a teacher or class

If you'd rather learn in a structured way, lessons are worth it. Many teachers will come to your home if you have three friends who want to learn together.

  • Adult and continuing education. School districts, community colleges and universities often run multi-week "Intro to Mah Jongg" courses.
  • Community centers and libraries. The places above frequently offer lessons as well as open play.
  • Teacher directories. Several American Mahjong websites and brands maintain lists of instructors by state. Check a teacher's rates, class size and whether they teach with the current NMJL card.
  • Ask local players. Once you find one group, ask who taught them.

Tournaments

Once you know the basics, tournaments are a fun way to play a lot of hands and meet other players. They range from small local events to multi-day gatherings.

  • Local events: community centers, clubs and charities often run one-day tournaments, sometimes as fundraisers. Local Facebook groups are the best place to hear about them.
  • Larger organizers: Several organizations run American Mahjong tournaments around the country and publish event calendars online. Search for "Mah Jongg tournament" with your state or the current year.

Tournaments usually have their own rules for timing, scoring and disputes, so read the event's rules before you go.

Before your first local game

  • Bring your current card. Most groups use the current NMJL card. Ask in advance if you're not sure.
  • Ask about house rules. Stakes, time limits and how strictly mistakes are called all vary.
  • Say you're new. Players are almost always happy to slow down for someone who tells them.

Want to get more comfortable first? Brush up with the beginner's guide or practice with an online game.
