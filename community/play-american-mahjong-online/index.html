Where to Play American Mahjong Online

Several platforms let you play American Mahjong with the Charleston, Jokers and the current card, against real people, computer players or both. They're the easiest way to practice between game nights.

Updated September 2026 / 3 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

Most Mahjong video games play Chinese or Japanese rules, or tile-matching solitaire. Finding a game that follows American rules, with Jokers, the Charleston and the annual card, takes a little more looking. Here's what's available and how to choose. (For in-person options, see finding a local game.)

Before you subscribe: Online platforms change their features, supported cards and prices often. We describe each one in general terms and don't list prices. Check the platform's own site for current details. Many platforms require you to own the current NMJL card to play with it.

What to look for

  • Current card support. Make sure the platform supports this year's NMJL card, or the card your group uses.
  • Real players, bots, or both. Bots let you practice at your own pace. Real players are more fun and more realistic.
  • Private tables. Useful if you want to play with your own group from different homes.
  • Learning tools. Hand suggestions and statistics help beginners learn the card faster.
  • Device support. Some platforms have phone and tablet apps. Others run in a web browser.

Platforms to consider

NMJL Online

The National Mah Jongg League's own official online game. You can play against other people or computer players, and there's a tutorial for new players. Playing requires the current physical card plus an online game membership purchased from the League. Available as an app, with details on the League's website.

Good for: players who want the League's official version.

Real Mah Jongg

A long-running platform for playing American Mahjong with the current card against live players, computer players or a mix. It includes hand suggestions for beginners, private play with friends, and voice and text chat. It also supports some alternative American cards. Available on the web and as a mobile app.

Good for: playing with the current card on a phone or tablet.

I Love Mahj

A browser-based platform built by American Mahjong players. It has computer players at several skill levels, a practice mode with hand suggestions, detailed personal statistics, card exercises, private tables and built-in video calls. The site also offers free learning material and tools for finding local groups. It offers a free trial.

Good for: beginners who want structured practice and progress tracking.

Mahjong 4 Friends

A free platform that plays American Mahjong (with NMJL and alternative cards) as well as Chinese, Hong Kong and British styles. You can play with friends, computer players or both, on the web or in its app, with in-game tips for new players.

Good for: casual practice without a subscription, or groups that play more than one style.

Mahjong Time

A large platform that hosts several rule sets, including American. It's popular with competitive players and runs online events. The interface has more going on than some alternatives, which can take getting used to.

Good for: players who also play other Mahjong styles, or who enjoy competition.

How to get the most from online practice

  • Start with bots. Computer players won't mind if you take your time on the Charleston.
  • Turn on hand suggestions early, then turn them off. Use them to learn the card, not to play for you.
  • Keep your physical card beside you. Reading the real card during online games makes it familiar faster.
  • Review your games. If the platform shows which hands you tend to play, try branching out to other sections.
