How to Teach a Complete Beginner on Their First Night

The goal of a first game night isn't perfect strategy. It's getting the new player comfortable enough to want to play again.

Updated September 2026 / 2 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

On this page

  1. 1. Play the first game open-handed
  2. 2. Give a five-minute overview, not a lecture
  3. 3. Choose one teacher
  4. 4. Narrate every move out loud
  5. 5. Skip scoring and slow down
  6. A simple first-night plan
  7. Expect a little mess

American Mahjong can feel like a fire hose of rules. If you try to teach all of it before the first tile is drawn, a beginner will be overwhelmed before the game starts. Strip it down to the basics and take the pressure of winning off the table. (Looking for other social guides? See Around the table.)

1. Play the first game open-handed

This is the single most useful thing you can do. For the first hand or two, have everyone place their tiles face-up on their racks.

Now you can point at actual tiles and explain what's happening:

  • "You have three 7 Dots, so you only need one more for a Kong."
  • "Nobody's collecting Winds, so that's a safe discard."
  • "See how your tiles fit this section of the card? Let's keep those."

Open play takes away the fear of doing something wrong in secret, and it prevents most dead hands before they happen. Once the new player understands the rhythm of the game, turn the tiles around.

2. Give a five-minute overview, not a lecture

People learn by playing. Skip the edge cases, penalties and payouts until later. Before the deal, cover only four things:

  1. The goal: build a 14-tile hand that exactly matches one line on the card.
  2. The turn: draw one tile, discard one tile.
  3. Jokers: wild in groups of three or more, never in singles or pairs.
  4. Calling: you can take someone's discard if it completes a group of three or more.

Everything else can wait until it comes up naturally. If they want to read ahead afterward, send them our beginner's guide.

3. Choose one teacher

If several experienced players are at the table, agree that only one of them does the teaching for the night. Three veterans offering different tips, house rules and strategy at the same time will confuse anyone.

The teacher guides the beginner's choices and makes the final call on rules questions. Everyone else plays and stays friendly.

4. Narrate every move out loud

Experienced players move quickly and quietly. Beginners need to hear what's happening. Ask everyone at the table to say their actions out loud:

"I'm drawing from the wall. That gives me three Bams, so I'm keeping them. I'll discard the 4 Crak."

It feels a little silly at first, but it turns every turn into a short lesson.

5. Skip scoring and slow down

For the first session, ignore values and money and play for fun. Take your time in the Charleston in particular. A new player needs time to compare tiles against the card, and that's where rushing hurts most.

A simple first-night plan

WhenWhat to do
Before the dealFive-minute overview. Show each tile type once. Point out the card's sections.
Hands 1 and 2Open-handed, fully narrated, no scoring. Teacher helps with every choice.
Hand 3Tiles face-down, still narrated. Teacher helps when asked.
Later handsNormal play. Introduce Joker exchanges and dead hands as they come up.

Expect a little mess

There will be knocked-over walls, forgotten discards and a tile or two exposed by accident. Laugh, reshuffle and keep going. Almost everyone feels lost for their first few games. The glossary helps with vocabulary afterward: 20 terms beginners should know.
