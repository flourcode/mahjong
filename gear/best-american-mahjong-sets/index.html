The Best American Mahjong Sets: A Buyer's Guide

Look for at least 152 tiles including 8 Jokers, four racks with built-in pushers, clear tile faces, and a case that suits how often you'll carry it.

Updated September 2026 / 4 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

Buying a first set is confusing mostly because Chinese and American sets look similar in listings. This guide explains what matters, then suggests a few well-known options. It's part of our gear guides.

How we chose

We researched sets using manufacturer specifications, retailer listings and what matters to players at the table. We haven't hands-on tested these sets. We compared:

  • Tile count and Jokers: at least 152 playable tiles including 8 Jokers
  • Readability: clear engraving, and corner numerals on suit tiles
  • Racks and pushers: built-in pushers preferred
  • Case: soft bag, hard case or wheeled case
  • Tile material: melamine or acrylic
  • Portability and value

What to check before you buy

Tile count and Jokers

An American set must include at least 152 tiles, with 8 Jokers. Many American sets list 166 tiles, which includes spares such as extra Jokers, extra Flowers and blanks. A 144-tile set is a Chinese set and won't work with the NMJL card. See why the sets differ.

Racks and pushers

Racks hold your tiles upright. Pushers slide your wall to the middle. Sets with "all-in-one" racks, where the pusher is built in, save assembly and missing pieces. Sets without pushers are workable but more awkward.

Readable tiles

Look for crisp, deep engraving and corner numbers on the suit tiles. This matters most for beginners and anyone who finds small print hard to read.

Material

Melamine is the classic choice for regular play. Acrylic is common in designer sets. Our tile materials guide compares them.

Case

Soft bags are lighter to carry. Hard aluminum cases protect tiles better. Wheeled cases help if you carry your set often.

Our picks

Best for beginners

Yellow Mountain Imports American Mahjong sets (soft case)

Yellow Mountain Imports is a long-established Mahjong retailer whose American sets are a common first purchase. Their soft-case American sets pair melamine tiles with a lightweight fabric bag, and many models include racks with pushers and dice. Check that the tile faces include corner numerals if readability matters to you.

  • Why it stands out: an established brand, easy to carry, suitable for regular play
  • Trade-off: a soft bag protects tiles less than a hard case

Best for durability and travel

White Swan American Mah Jongg sets

White Swan sets come in hard aluminum cases, including luggage-style and wheeled versions. Retailer listings describe engraved melamine tiles, 166 tiles in total including spares, and all-in-one racks with pushers. The padded case with separate tile trays keeps everything organized in transit.

  • Why it stands out: sturdy case, built-in pushers, wheeled options for frequent travel
  • Trade-off: heavier and pricier than soft-case sets. At the time of our research, listings were roughly in the $190 to $220 range.

Best on a budget

ZenSports American Mahjong set

ZenSports sells an American set with all-in-one racks and pushers and a padded bag, typically priced below the premium brands. It's a reasonable way to find out whether your group will stick with the game before spending more.

  • Why it stands out: complete American set with built-in pushers at a lower price
  • Trade-off: budget sets can vary more in finish and engraving. Check recent listings and product photos.

Best if looks matter

Designer acrylic sets

A growing number of brands sell designer American sets with colorful, layered or patterned acrylic tile backs. They make striking gifts and look beautiful on the table. Before buying, confirm the tile count, that Jokers are included, and whether racks and pushers come with the set or are sold separately.

  • Why it stands out: style and gift appeal
  • Trade-off: usually more expensive, and acrylic is more prone to fine surface scratches than melamine

Set questions

Why do American sets have 166 tiles?

Play uses 152 tiles. The extra tiles are spares, typically additional Jokers, Flowers and blank tiles to replace any that go missing. Set them aside before you play.

What are the blank tiles for?

They're replacements. If a tile is lost, you can mark a blank to stand in for it. Some groups have used blanks as extra Jokers in the past, but standard play uses 8 Jokers.

Does a set come with the NMJL card?

Some sets include an old card or rule booklet, but you'll need the current year's card, ordered separately from the League.

What else will I need?

A mat is the most useful extra. Then just a table and three friends. Our setup guide takes you from there.
