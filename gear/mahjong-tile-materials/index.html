Melamine vs. Acrylic vs. Porcelain Mahjong Tiles

Melamine is the practical choice for regular play. Acrylic is popular for designer sets. Porcelain sets are mostly decorative and aren't well suited to heavy play.

Updated September 2026 / 2 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

Tile material affects how a set sounds, feels and holds up. Every hand starts with washing, where all the tiles are mixed face-down, so the tiles rub against each other and the table constantly. It's worth choosing a material that suits how often you'll play. (For full set recommendations, see our set buying guide.)

Melamine: the everyday standard

Melamine is a hard, heat-resistant plastic, and it's what most club and tournament sets use.

  • Feel: dense and substantial. Tiles sit steadily on the rack.
  • Sound: the deep, satisfying clack many people associate with Mahjong.
  • Durability: melamine is generally very durable and resistant to ordinary game-night wear. With reasonable care, a good set can last for many years of regular play.
  • Looks: opaque, usually bright white or ivory.

Acrylic: the designer choice

Acrylic has become popular for premium and designer sets because it can be clear, colored or layered in ways melamine can't.

  • Feel: often a little lighter than melamine, with crisper edges.
  • Sound: still pleasant, a little higher and lighter.
  • Durability: acrylic is somewhat softer than melamine, so the faces and backs can pick up fine surface scratches over time.
  • Looks: the main appeal. Colors, glitter, foil and artwork can be built into the tile backs.

Porcelain and ceramic: mostly for display

Some people assume "authentic" sets are porcelain. Historically, fine antique sets were more often made from materials like bone and bamboo. Porcelain sets exist today, but they're mainly decorative.

  • Feel: heavy and cool, like fine china.
  • Sound: a sharp, glassy clink that some find harsh during a long wash.
  • Durability: ceramic can chip or crack when tiles knock together during washing or fall off the table.

If a listing says "porcelain," check whether it's truly ceramic or a plastic tile with a porcelain-style design. The difference matters if you plan to play with it.

Caring for your tiles

  • Play on a mat to reduce wear and noise.
  • Keep sticky and greasy snacks away from the table. See our hosting guide for tile-friendly food.
  • Wipe tiles with a slightly damp cloth and dry them. Avoid soaking them or using harsh cleaners, and follow the maker's advice.
  • Store tiles in their trays, away from direct heat.

Which should you choose?

If you'll play weekly, choose melamine. If the set is a gift or a showpiece and will get lighter use, acrylic is a lovely option. Choose porcelain only for display.
