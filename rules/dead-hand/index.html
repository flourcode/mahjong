The Dead Hand: What It Means in American Mahjong

A dead hand can no longer win. It usually comes from a structural mistake: the wrong number of tiles, an exposure that matches no hand on the card, or an illegal use of a Joker.

Updated September 2026 / 3 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

Quick answer: A hand is dead when it can no longer legally win, usually because of the wrong tile count, an impossible or illegal exposure, a Joker used in a pair or single, or a false Mahjong. The player stops playing that hand but generally still pays the winner.

Hearing "your hand is dead" is practically a rite of passage in American Mahjong. It rarely means anyone cheated. It usually means someone lost count or misread the card. Knowing the common causes is the best way to avoid them. This is part of our rules guides.

What happens when your hand is dead

  • You stop drawing and discarding for the rest of that hand.
  • You can't win that hand.
  • You stay at the table, and if your group plays for money, you generally still pay the winner.
  • Your tiles stay on your rack. You don't push them into the middle.

The other players continue. When the hand ends, you're back in for the next one.

The five common causes

1. The wrong number of tiles

You should hold 13 tiles between turns, and 14 just after you draw. If it turns out you have more or fewer, the hand is usually dead.

How it happens: forgetting to discard after drawing, drawing twice, or a tile falling off your rack unnoticed.

2. An impossible exposure

Every group you expose has to fit at least one hand on the current card. If you expose a group that no hand on the card could use, your hand can't win, so it's dead.

Example: you call a discard and expose a Kong of a tile that no hand on this year's card uses as a Kong.

3. Exposing for a concealed hand

Hands marked C on the card can't use called discards, except the winning tile. If you call a discard and expose a group, and the only hands that group could fit are concealed ones, your hand is dead. See X vs. C on the card.

4. Illegal Joker use

Jokers can't be used in a pair or a single. Exposing a pair of North Winds made from one North and one Joker, for example, is illegal. See the Joker rules.

5. A false Mahjong

If you declare Mahjong and lay down your hand, and it doesn't actually match a line on the card, it's a false Mahjong. Your hand is dead and play generally continues with the others.

Don't throw in your tiles too fast. If someone declares Mahjong, check their hand against the card before you expose or mix in your own tiles. If the Mahjong turns out to be false and you've already thrown in your hand, you may lose your hand too. Exact procedures for this vary, so agree on them at your table.

Who can call a hand dead?

Any player can point out that a hand looks dead, and it should be said out loud rather than kept quiet. Play pauses while the table checks. The player whose hand is questioned can concede or show a hand on the card that their tiles could still legally make.

Some groups add their own etiquette here, such as only raising it on your own turn, and tournaments have their own procedures. In a friendly game, the goal is to be fair and keep things moving.

What happens to exposed Jokers

  • Legal exposures made before the mistake generally stay face-up, and other players can still exchange for Jokers in them.
  • The illegal exposure itself is generally returned to the player's rack, so its Jokers can't be taken.
  • For a concealed-hand error, exposures are generally all returned, so no Jokers can be taken.

Treat these as common practice rather than universal law. Official materials and tournament rules are the authority for formal play.

How to avoid dead hands

  • Count your tiles every few turns, and always after calling a tile.
  • Check the card before you call. Make sure at least one exposed hand on the card uses the group you're about to expose.
  • Never use a Joker in a pair or single.
  • Check the whole hand before calling Mahjong, including the note in parentheses.

If you're teaching someone, a dead hand is a great learning moment rather than a failure. Our guide on teaching a beginner suggests playing the first game open-handed, which prevents most of these mistakes.
