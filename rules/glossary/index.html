American Mahjong Glossary: 20 Terms Beginners Should Know

Most of the confusion at a new table is vocabulary. Learn these twenty words and the rest of the conversation starts to make sense.

Updated September 2026 / 3 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

These terms are grouped roughly in the order you'll meet them: the equipment, the tiles, groups of tiles, and then gameplay. If you're brand new, start with our beginner's guide to the game and come back here whenever a word doesn't make sense.

The terms

NMJL
The National Mah Jongg League, the organization that publishes the annual card and official rules for American Mahjong as most people play it in the US.
The card
The NMJL's annual list of winning hands. A new one comes out every year, usually in spring, and only hands on the current card can win. See how to read the card.
Rack and pusher
The rack holds your tiles upright and hidden. The pusher is the arm, often attached to the rack, used to slide your wall toward the center of the table.
Wall
The double-stacked row of face-down tiles each player builds before a hand. In American Mahjong each wall is 19 tiles long and 2 high. Players draw from the walls in order.
Suits: Bams, Craks, Dots
The three numbered suits, 1 to 9. Bams are Bamboo, Craks are Characters, and Dots are circles.
Honors
The tiles without numbers: the four Winds (North, East, West, South) and the three Dragons (Red, Green, White).
Joker
A wild tile unique to American Mahjong. There are 8 in a set. A Joker can replace any tile in a group of three or more, but never a single tile or a pair. See Joker rules.
Soap
Nickname for the White Dragon, whose face often looks like an empty rectangle. It also counts as a zero in hands that spell out a year.
Pair
Two identical tiles. Jokers can never be used in a pair.
Pung
Three identical tiles.
Kong
Four identical tiles.
Quint
Five identical tiles. Since a set only has four of any tile, a Quint always needs at least one Joker.
Sextet
Six identical tiles. It needs at least two Jokers.
The Charleston
The tile-passing phase before regular play. Players pass three tiles at a time in a fixed order: right, across, left, then optionally left, across, right. See how the Charleston works.
Blind pass
On the last pass of each Charleston, a player who doesn't want to give up three of their own tiles may pass along some or all of the tiles arriving in that pass without looking at them. Also called stealing.
Courtesy pass
An optional final trade of zero to three tiles with the player sitting across from you, after the Charleston is over.
Call (or expose)
Claiming another player's discard to complete a group of three or more (or to win). You place the finished group face-up on top of your rack, which is called exposing it.
Concealed hand
A hand marked C on the card. You must build it entirely from the wall and can't call any discards, except the tile that completes the hand for Mahjong.
Joker exchange
On your turn, you can give a player the real tile their exposed Joker is standing in for and take the Joker. Sometimes called redeeming a Joker.
Dead hand
A hand that can no longer win because of a mistake, such as the wrong tile count or an impossible exposure. The player stops playing that hand but usually still pays the winner. See dead hand rules.

Common questions about the terms

What is Soap in Mahjong?

Soap is the White Dragon. On many sets it's a plain or outlined rectangle that looks a bit like a bar of soap. In American Mahjong it's both a Dragon and the zero in year hands.

What's the difference between a Pung and a Kong?

A Pung is three identical tiles and a Kong is four. American Mahjong also has Quints (five) and Sextets (six), which are only possible with Jokers.

What does concealed mean?

A concealed hand, marked C on the card, has to be built from tiles you draw yourself. You can't call other players' discards for it, except the very last tile that wins the hand. Hands marked X (exposed) let you call discards.

Is "Mah Jongg" spelled differently from "Mahjong"?

Both spellings are common. The National Mah Jongg League uses "Mah Jongg," and many American players do too. We use "Mahjong" on this site because it's the most widely searched spelling, but they mean the same game.
