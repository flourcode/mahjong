How to Set Up an American Mahjong Game

Wash the tiles, build four walls of 19 tiles two high, roll for East, break the wall and deal until East has 14 tiles and everyone else has 13.

Updated September 2026 / 3 min read / MahjongCenter Editorial Team

Quick answer: Each player builds a wall 19 tiles long and 2 tiles high. The dealer (East) starts with 14 tiles, and the other three players start with 13.

Setup is the same at the start of every hand, so after a few games you'll do it without thinking. This is part of our beginner's guide to American Mahjong.

What you need on the table

  • An American Mahjong set with at least 152 tiles, including 8 Jokers
  • Four racks, ideally with pushers
  • Two dice
  • A current card for each player
  • A square table. A standard card table works well. A mat quiets the tiles and protects the surface.

Most American sets include more than 152 tiles, usually extra Jokers, Flowers and blanks as spares. Set the extras aside before you start so the count is right.

1. Wash the tiles

Turn every tile face-down in the middle of the table. Everyone uses both hands to swirl them around. This is washing, and it's the shuffle. Keep going until the tiles are well mixed. Fifteen to twenty seconds is usually plenty.

Keep tiles face-down the whole time. If a tile flips over, turn it back and mix it in.

2. Build the walls

  1. Make a row of 19 tiles, face-down, side by side in front of your rack.
  2. Make a second row of 19 and stack it on top of the first. Your wall is now 19 tiles long and 2 tiles high: 38 tiles.
  3. Push the wall forward with your pusher, angled so the four walls form a square in the middle of the table.

Four walls of 38 tiles is exactly 152. If someone ends up with a short or long wall, recount. Something is off, and it's much easier to fix now than later.

?

Beginner tip: Building a 19-tile row is easier if you make it in small groups, like four groups of four plus three, then slide them together.

If you've played Chinese Mahjong, you may be used to shorter walls of 18 tiles, since those sets have 144 tiles. The extra stack in American walls comes from the 8 Jokers. More on that in Chinese vs. American Mahjong.

3. Choose the dealer (East)

The dealer is called East. A common way to choose is for each player to roll the two dice. The highest total becomes East. Ties roll again.

Seats then take the other Wind names going around the table to the right: the player to East's right is South, across is West, and to East's left is North.

After each hand, the role of East usually moves to the next player on the right.

4. Break the wall

East rolls the dice again. In NMJL-style play, East then counts that many stacks from the right end of their own wall and separates the wall at that point. That gap is where the deal begins.

House rules vary: Some tables, especially players who learned Chinese Mahjong first, count around the table to decide whose wall to break. Either works as long as the whole table agrees before the dice are rolled.

5. Deal the tiles

  1. East takes the first four tiles (two stacks) from the wall next to the break.
  2. Each player to the right takes four tiles in turn, continuing along the wall.
  3. Repeat until each player has 12 tiles.
  4. Finish the deal: East takes two more tiles and each of the others takes one.

When the deal is done, East has 14 tiles and everyone else has 13. Everyone arranges their tiles on their racks, checks the card and gets ready for the Charleston.

Some groups deal in a slightly different pattern for the final tiles. What matters is the result: 14 for East, 13 for everyone else.

Setup questions

How many tiles does each player start with?

East starts with 14 and the other three players start with 13. East starts regular play by discarding a tile after the Charleston, which brings them to 13 like everyone else.

How long is an American Mahjong wall?

19 tiles long and 2 tiles high, so 38 tiles per player and 152 in total.

Who starts as East?

Commonly, whoever rolls the highest total with two dice. After that, East usually rotates to the right after each hand.

Can you play with fewer than four players?

American Mahjong is designed for four. Some groups play three-player or two-player variations with adjusted rules, but these aren't standard, so agree on the details first.
