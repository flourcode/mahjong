American Mahjong strategy for beginners

Good strategy in American Mahjong starts with flexibility. Stay open early, let your real tiles guide your choice, and watch what the rest of the table is showing you.

Start with the Charleston

Many hands are shaped before the first tile is drawn from the wall. If you only study one strategy topic, make it this.

↔StrategyThe CharlestonThe right-across-left passing sequence, without the panic.

Six beginner principles

1. Pick a section before a line

Look at your dealt tiles and decide which one or two sections of the card they fit. Keep two or three candidate lines in mind and let the Charleston and your early draws narrow it down. How the card is organized.

2. Let real pairs guide you

Jokers can't fill a pair or a single, so the pairs and singles in a hand have to come from real tiles. If a hand needs a pair you don't have, it's harder than it looks. If a hand needs a pair you already hold, it's often a good bet.

3. Prefer exposed hands while you're learning

Hands marked X let you call discards to complete groups, which gives you more ways to finish. Concealed hands (C) must be built from the wall. They're worth more on the card, but they're harder for new players.

4. Know when to switch

If the tiles you need are being discarded by others, or appear in someone's exposed groups, your hand may be getting harder. Switching early, while your tiles still fit another line, usually beats chasing a hand that can't come in.

5. Read the exposures

When another player exposes a group, it narrows down which hands they could be building. Glance at the card and ask which sections use that group. Then think about which tiles they still need.

6. Discard more carefully late in the hand

As the wall gets short, some discards become riskier. A few habits help:

  • Tiles that have already been discarded, or that no exposed hand seems to need, are generally safer.
  • Tiles that match the suits, numbers or Winds in a player's exposures are generally riskier, especially if that player has several groups showing.
  • If you can no longer realistically win, it's reasonable to hold a tile that looks dangerous and discard something safer instead.
A rough guide to discards late in a hand
Generally saferGenerally riskier
A tile already discarded by someone elseA tile that fits a player's exposed groups
A suit no one appears to be collectingA suit that shows up in several exposures
Winds or Dragons no exposed hand seems to useWinds or Dragons when someone has exposed Winds or Dragons
Early in the handLate in the hand, when players are close to finishing

No discard is ever completely safe. These are tendencies, not guarantees.
